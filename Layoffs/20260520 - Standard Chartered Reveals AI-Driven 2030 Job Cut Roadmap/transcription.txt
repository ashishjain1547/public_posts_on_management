The global layoff wave is no longer limited just to Silicon Valley. Now, one of the world's biggest international banks is preparing deep job cuts as artificial intelligence reshapes the workplace. Standard Chartered is preparing a major restructuring drive. The bank is pushing deeper into artificial intelligence and automation. It plans to reduce more than 15% of its corporate function roles by 2030. That could result in more than 7,000 job cuts across its global operations. The cuts are expected to mainly affect back office and support service positions. The bank currently employs around 80,000 people worldwide. Standard Chartered has a strong presence across the Asia-Pacific, West Asia, and Africa. It is also the largest international foreign bank operating in India. Standard Chartered currently runs around 100 branches across 43 Indian cities. The bank has spent years restructuring its operations after periods of weak performance and growing regulatory pressure. The push comes as competition intensifies, and global companies search for greater efficiency. While outlining its future business plans to investors, Standard Chartered raised its long-term profit targets. The bank said it expects to deliver more than 15% returns by the year 2028, and that figure is expected to rise to 18% by 2030. Executives believe restructuring and growth in higher-margin businesses will help drive that expansion. The lender's Hong Kong-listed shares also moved higher after this announcement. Now, Standard Chartered CEO Bill Winters said the reductions would be driven by automation and wider adoption of artificial intelligence. Our strategy is grounded in a simple belief. The world is becoming more connected, more complex, and more cross-border. Clients need a bank that can help them navigate that environment with confidence. That is where Standard Chartered is distinctive. Our trusted ability to combine network and product capabilities to solve challenging cross-border problems is difficult to replicate. Now, the London-headquartered bank says it has already achieved some of its earlier financial targets ahead of schedule. Attention is now shifting to whether the bank can sustain that momentum in an uncertain global environment. We achieved our 2026 medium-term financial targets a year earlier than planned. We now have a more focused, streamlined, and efficient organization positioning us strongly for the next stage of growth and to deliver our strategy at greater scale and pace. While Standard Chartered, some employees are expected to re-skill as the bank expands automation and AI-led operations, the lender says the changes are part of a broader effort to streamline operations and improve efficiency. The announcement also comes at a time of growing geopolitical tensions. Financial markets across several regions continue to face pressure from global conflicts and economic uncertainty. Analysts believe Asia-Pacific banks could face higher loan-loss provisions if the West Asia conflict continues. Rising energy prices and slower economic growth are also adding to those concerns. Standard Chartered had already set aside precautionary provisions linked to the conflict earlier this year. The bank's latest move also reflects a broader trend across major global companies. Technology firms such as Meta have also announced restructuring plans linked to automation. The company recently outlined layoffs and internal changes amid and aimed at reducing management layers and building faster workflows. For Standard Chartered, this is more than a restructuring exercise. It is another sign that artificial intelligence is steadily replacing work once done by humans, even inside global banking giants.
